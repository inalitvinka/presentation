Scrum Framework

Understanding Agile at work

📝 Hi there! We will talk about the Scrum Framework — one of the most popular ways teams use Agile in real work. Agile started as a way to build software faster and better by focusing on people, collaboration, and quick feedback. It helps teams stay organized, adapt to change, and achive goals step by step.

What is Scrum?

  • A lightweight Agile framework
  • Focused on delivering value through iterative work
  • The history of Scrum
📝 Scrum is an agile project management framework. It helps teams structure and manage their work through a set of values, principles, and practices. Scrum helps teams adapt quickly by breaking work into small, manageable time-boxed units called sprints. It fosters continuous improvement and collaboration. You might wonder — why is it even called Scrum? The name actually comes from rugby. In a rugby "scrum," the team huddles together and pushes forward as one unit. This idea really inspired the Scrum creators — they wanted development teams to work the same way: closely, collaboratively, and moving forward together instead of passing work around in a rigid sequence.

Scrum Roles

  • Product Owner
  • Scrum Master
  • Development Team
📝The Scrum team is everyone who is working on the sprint. Team members should be self-organizing and collaborative in order to achieve the Scrum goal of continuous improvement. In Scrum, team members are assigned to three specific roles: product owner, Scrum master, and the development team. These roles are used to assign specific tasks to team members.

Product Owner

Defines priorities and ROI

📝Product Owner is the person in charge of the product backlog. He stays in touch with users’ needs, helps the team understand what matters most, and makes sure the product is heading in the right direction. He is usually the one who says, “Yes, this is ready to go live!”

Scrum Master

Facilitates the process and removes blockers

📝The scrum master, as the title implies, is the master of scrum, who ensures the scrum framework is followed. He is the person running the various Scrum events. Think of him as the Scrum project manager and facilitator. The Scrum master should facilitate the daily standup meetings and host the sprint planning, review, and retrospective meetings.

Developers

Cross-functional team

📝The development team is responsible for creating the product. And because scrum teams are cross-functional, the development team includes testers, designers, UX specialists, and ops engineers in addition to developers.

Scrum Ceremonies

  • Sprint Planning
  • Daily Stand‑up
  • Sprint Review
  • Sprint Retrospective
📝 Scrum emphasizes structured, regular communication through ceremonies. Each has a clear purpose and cadence. Let’s walk through them all.
📝Traditionally, Scrum runs in sprints, which are usually two-week-long working sessions.
Sprint Planning takes place at the beginning of each sprint. It usually lasts about 1 hour per week of sprint. This meeting is used to break work into tasks and discuss how they will be done. The Product Owner presents a backlog, and the team estimates effort. Everyone should leave the meeting with a clear understanding of the sprint goal and plan.
Daily standups (Daily Scrum) happen once a day. These are an opportunity for the Scrum team to connect for 15 minutes and coordinate daily activities. Each team member answers three quick questions: 1. What did I accomplish yesterday? 2. What will I work on today? 3. Is there anything blocking me? Duration is around 45 minutes per week of sprint.
Sprint Review takes place at the end of the sprint. The development team demonstrates the work completed to stakeholders to collect feedback and adjust priorities.
Sprint Retrospective is a final ceremony of the sprint. The team looks what worked and what didn’t. It’s about team processes, collaboration, tools, and what to improve. Duration is usually the same as the sprint review.

Three Pillars of Scrum

  • Transparency
  • Inspection
  • Adaptation
📝 Now, when ceremonies are clear, let’s look at the three pillars of Scrum — Transparency, Inspection, and Adaptation.
Transparency means that all key aspects of the process are visible and understandable to all participants.
Inspection involves frequent and regular checks on both progress and process. These happen during events like Sprint Planning, Daily Stand‑ups, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective. Inspection helps spot problems early and find better ways to work.
Adaptation is about adjusting strategy, plans, or execution based on insights gained through inspection.

Three pillars form an empirical feedback loop

📝 Together, three pillars form an empirical feedback loop. Transparency reveals reality. Inspection evaluates it. Adaptation acts on results. This cycle enables teams to learn continuously and align with goals.

Scrum Artifacts

  • Product Backlog
  • Sprint Backlog
  • Increment
📝 To support this continuous feedback loop, Scrum provides artifacts that help teams stay aligned and transparent.
The product backlog is a continuously updated, prioritized list of features, improvements, bug fixes, and tasks needed to develop the product.
The sprint backlog is a list of items selected from the product backlog, which the team plans to complete during the sprint. These items are broken down into smaller tasks that clarify what needs to be done. The sprint backlog is created during sprint planning and updated during the sprint. It helps teams organize their work, assign tasks, and track progress. If the team can't complete all tasks, some remain in the backlog for future sprints.
The increment is everything completed during the sprint, along with what was done in previous sprints. Each increment is a working version of the product that meets the team’s definition of “done.” It shows progress and helps the team and stakeholders see what has been built.

Metrics & Tracking Tools

  • Team velocity
  • Sprint burndown chart
📝 Scrum teams often use metrics like velocity (how much work is typically completed in a sprint) and tools like the burndown chart (which shows remaining work over time) to track performance, plan future sprints, or adjust goals.

Why Scrum works?

📝 Scrum works because it’s based on real-world experience and quick learning. Teams manage themselves, check progress often, and make changes when needed. This helps them stay focused, improve constantly, and deliver better results.
